Consumer Checkpoint Surveillance pricing uses our personal data to make us pay more Personalized pricing can mean unfair, and even biased, prices Surveillance pricing leverages data collected from our web history , smart devices , and apps with the goal of getting us to pay more Surveillance pricing versus dynamic pricing Surveillance pricing, according to the nonprofit Electronic Privacy Information Center ( EPIC ), is an algorithm pricing practice that charges individuals or groups of individuals different prices “for the same goods or services.” “The goal of surveillance pricing is to infer the highest price that the customer is willing to pay, based on consumers’ personal data and other market data,” EPIC explains Dynamic pricing , or surge pricing, differs from surveillance pricing, in that, instead of relying on consumer data, it uses changing market trends, like supply and demand, inventory levels, and competitor pricing, to set prices Khan “… retailers frequently use people’s personal information to set targeted, tailored prices for goods and services—from a person’s location and demographics, down to their mouse movements on a webpage.” Surveillance pricing has become more widespread as food delivery platforms, rideshare services, rental housing providers, and other businesses have gained access to the technology and data needed to implement it Instacart previously practiced surveillance pricing, also called personalized pricing
