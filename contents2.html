Meta’s Superintelligence Lab released a pair of new state-of-the-art models on Monday, Muse Wonder and Muse Wonder Full, representing the company’s first attempt at a frontier model. Where Muse Spark aimed to be a lightweight model that could perform at low latency, the Wonder models are larger and said to be more capable, with higher benchmark scores and better results on complex tasks like coding. According to Meta’s testing (presented in the model card ), Wonder and Wonder Full achieve comparable benchmark scores to Fable 5.1 and GPT-6 Astra. “Together, they mark the next step on our scaling ladder and a major leap toward personal superintelligence,” Meta’s blog post about the models reads. That scale is reflected in the pricing: Accessed through the Meta Model API, Wonder will cost $4 per million input tokens and $20 per million output tokens, making it roughly equivalent in cost to GPT-6 Sol or Opus 5.5. Notably, Wonder Full will only be available to Muse Code subscribers and will not be served through the API. Both models will also be used to power the Muse agent. In keeping with Meta’s interest in developing personal assistants that can perform tasks over a long period, the company placed particular emphasis on the new models’ ability to retain and synthesize information given by the user, a property Meta calls “active memory.” To achieve better recall, Meta highlighted the MemoryBench benchmark , which challenges continual learning and memory construction frameworks. According to the company’s testing, Muse Wonder now scores higher on MemoryBench than Fable, Opus, or Astra. “We put a tremendous amount of effort into making sure your model can remember what’s important to you over days, weeks, months, and beyond,” said research scientist Kavya Kopparapu in a video accompanying the launch. Big Tech Meta Meta says it's caught up to the frontier with its latest Wonder AI models The models add "active memory" and "active focus" abilities. By Karissa Bell Oct. 12, 2026 2:51 pm EST Smith Collection/gado/Getty Images Add Engadget on Google: Preferred Source Google Discover After spending more than a year and tens of billions of dollars rebuilding its AI efforts, Meta says that it has caught up with the frontier. The company introduced its two latest AI models, Muse Wonder and Muse Wonder Full, both of which represent "a major leap toward personal superintelligence." According to Meta, the models are particularly adept at navigating legal, financial and health-related tasks, and are able to deliver "frontier coding performance." Both models also add "active memory" and "active focus," which Meta says will help people accomplish more over long periods of time. That's notable because Meta says it plans to bring Wonder to its personal agent Muse , beginning with paid subscribers. Active memory, according to the company, is "the ability to carry what matters to you across conversations and bring back the details that are relevant to the task at hand" while active focus will allow an agent to "keep working toward your goal when a task gets hard." The models are a notable milestone for Meta, which has said it expects to spend as much as $145 billion this year to fuel its ambitious AI plans. In an interview with the Financial Times , CEO Mark Zuckerberg said that "getting to this point both required a large amount of compute and investment, and a lot of very rigorous scientific work" from the company's superintelligence team. The new models are rolling out now to subscribers. Muse Wonder is available via Meta's API and in Muse Code. It's priced at $4 per million input tokens and $20 per million output tokens. Muse Wonder Full is available to people who subscribe to the $50/month "power usage" or $200/month "maximum usage" tiers of Muse Code.
